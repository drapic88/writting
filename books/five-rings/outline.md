# Outline — Five Rings

## The arc

Part I settles what the work is and what it is for, because every judgement later in the book
depends on it. Part II is the practitioner alone — attention, grip, and the difference between a fix
that works and a fix that resolves. Part III is the longest, and it is the book's centre: what to do
in the moment of engagement, when something is on fire or stuck or about to be migrated. Part IV
turns outward to how methods decay, which is the part a reader can hand to someone who has never
opened Musashi. Part V is short and cannot be read first.

A reader who stops after Part III has the whole practical book and has got their money's worth. Parts
IV and V are what make it worth keeping on the shelf.
